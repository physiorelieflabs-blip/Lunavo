# TS COMMERCE — CRITICAL PAYMENT + REFERRAL IMPLEMENTATION INSTRUCTIONS

## READ THIS FIRST

The most important instruction in this document is:

**DO NOT BUILD A FAKE PAYMENT SYSTEM.**

Do not simply create a checkout page saying "Payment successful."

Do not create fake bank accounts.

Do not create fake balances.

Do not assume that a TS Commerce code is a bank account.

Do not assume that Flutterwave sends money directly into a TS Commerce database.

You must understand the difference between:

1. A real payment destination
2. A payment provider
3. TS Pay
4. A TS Commerce internal ledger
5. A merchant balance
6. A bank/settlement account
7. An identifier/code

Build them as separate concepts.

---

# PART A — UNDERSTAND HOW MONEY ACTUALLY MOVES

## 1. TS PAY IS THE SOFTWARE CONTROL LAYER

TS Pay is TS Commerce's first-party payment operating layer.

It is responsible for:

* Creating payment sessions
* Creating orders
* Connecting to payment providers
* Tracking payment status
* Verifying payments
* Recording transactions
* Calculating TS Commerce fees
* Recording merchant earnings
* Recording refunds
* Recording disputes
* Recording settlements
* Maintaining the internal financial ledger
* Handling payout requests
* Reconciling provider transactions

However:

**TS Pay does NOT magically create a real bank account.**

A real bank account or virtual account must come from an actual banking/payment infrastructure provider.

For the first implementation, that provider is Flutterwave.

---

# PART B — THE ACTUAL CUSTOMER PAYMENT FLOW

Suppose a customer is buying a product for:

**₦50,000**

The customer reaches the merchant's TS Commerce storefront.

They click:

**Buy Now**

Then:

**Checkout**

Then:

**Payment**

TS Commerce creates:

* Order ID
* TS Pay payment session
* Unique payment reference
* Customer ID
* Merchant ID
* Store ID
* Amount
* Currency

For example:

Order:

`TS-ORDER-12345`

Payment reference:

`TS-PAY-98765`

These identifiers are created by TS Commerce.

They are NOT bank accounts.

---

# PART C — CUSTOMER CHOOSES BANK TRANSFER

Suppose the customer chooses:

**Bank Transfer**

At this point, do NOT tell the customer to transfer money to:

`TS-STORE-123`

or:

`TS-CUST-123`

or:

`TS-PAY-123`

Those are not bank accounts.

Instead:

TS Pay asks the configured payment provider, currently Flutterwave, to create/provide the appropriate bank-transfer payment destination.

If the Flutterwave payment flow returns a dynamically generated virtual account, Flutterwave returns the actual account information.

For example:

**Bank Name:** Example Bank
**Account Name:** Flutterwave / Provider-generated name
**Account Number:** 1234567890
**Amount:** ₦50,000
**Reference:** TS-PAY-98765
**Expires:** 30 minutes

THESE VALUES MUST COME FROM THE ACTUAL PROVIDER RESPONSE.

Do not invent them.

Do not hard-code them.

Do not create fake account numbers in the database.

Do not assume every Flutterwave transaction has the same account number.

Do not assume every payment method/country/currency has the same behavior.

---

# PART D — WHAT THE CUSTOMER ACTUALLY DOES

The customer's screen should clearly say something similar to:

**Bank Transfer**

Transfer exactly:

**₦50,000**

To:

**Bank:** [provider-returned bank name]

**Account Name:** [provider-returned account name]

**Account Number:** [provider-returned account number]

**Payment Reference:** [TS Pay reference]

**Expires:** [provider-returned expiration, if available]

Then:

**The customer opens their bank app and transfers ₦50,000 to the actual account number shown above.**

That is where the money actually goes.

The customer does NOT transfer money to the TS Store Code.

The customer does NOT transfer money to the Customer Code.

The customer does NOT transfer money to the TS Pay reference.

Those values only help TS Commerce identify and reconcile the payment.

---

# PART E — WHAT HAPPENS AFTER THE CUSTOMER TRANSFERS

This is extremely important.

The frontend must NOT say:

"Payment successful"

just because the customer clicked a button.

Instead:

Customer transfers money.

↓

Flutterwave/provider receives/detects the transfer.

↓

Flutterwave sends the appropriate event/webhook and/or makes the transaction available for verification.

↓

TS Commerce receives the provider event.

↓

TS Commerce verifies the webhook authenticity.

↓

TS Pay asks Flutterwave to verify/re-query the actual transaction.

↓

TS Pay checks:

* Provider transaction ID
* Amount
* Currency
* Status
* Payment reference
* Merchant
* Store
* Customer
* Order
* Payment session
* Duplicate status
* Refund/reversal status where applicable

↓

Only after successful verification:

**TS Pay marks the payment as successful.**

↓

The order becomes paid.

↓

The internal TS Pay ledger records the money.

↓

The merchant's dashboard balance is updated according to the applicable funds-availability/settlement rules.

This is the correct concept.

---

# PART F — DO NOT CONFUSE A LEDGER WITH A BANK ACCOUNT

This is another critical concept.

Suppose the customer pays:

**₦50,000**

Flutterwave may receive/settle the actual funds according to Flutterwave's infrastructure and settlement rules.

TS Commerce then records the verified transaction in its internal TS Pay ledger.

The merchant dashboard may show:

**Available balance: ₦50,000**

or an appropriate available/pending amount depending on settlement rules.

That dashboard number is an **internal accounting representation**.

It does NOT mean TS Commerce has magically created a bank account containing ₦50,000.

The application ledger and actual regulated funds/settlement infrastructure must remain separate concepts.

Never tell the user that a database balance itself is a bank account.

---

# PART G — FEES

Suppose:

Customer pays:

**₦50,000**

TS Commerce must separately calculate and record:

* Gross transaction: ₦50,000
* Flutterwave/provider fee: actual provider fee
* TS Commerce transaction fee: 1%
* Merchant net
* Settlement amount/currency
* Any applicable tax/shipping/discount
* Any FX information

Do NOT simply change ₦50,000 to a random smaller number.

Every deduction must have a reason and ledger entry.

For example:

Gross:

₦50,000

TS Commerce 1%:

₦500

Provider fee:

[actual provider-calculated fee]

Merchant net:

[calculated from actual verified values]

The exact provider fee must not be invented.

# PART I — FLUTTERWAVE WEBHOOKS

Never trust a webhook blindly.

When Flutterwave sends a webhook:

1. Receive it.
2. Verify its authenticity/signature.
3. Check the event ID.
4. Check whether the event has already been processed.
5. Re-query/verify the transaction with Flutterwave.
6. Compare the verified transaction against the TS Pay payment session.
7. Check amount.
8. Check currency.
9. Check reference.
10. Check merchant.
11. Check order.
12. Check customer.
13. Check whether the transaction was already credited.
14. Commit the ledger update transactionally.

If something does not match:

**DO NOT CREDIT THE MERCHANT.**

Create a reconciliation exception.

---

# PART J — DUPLICATE PAYMENT PROTECTION

Imagine Flutterwave sends the same webhook three times.

The merchant must NOT receive three credits.

Example:

Customer pays:

₦50,000

Webhook arrives:

Event 1 → credit ₦50,000

Event 2 → do nothing

Event 3 → do nothing

The final merchant ledger must contain only one valid credit.

Use:

* Provider transaction ID
* Event ID
* Payment reference
* Idempotency key
* Unique database constraints
* Database transactions
* Appropriate locks/version checks

---

# PART K — FRONTEND PAYMENT BUTTONS MUST NOT CONTROL MONEY

A frontend button saying:

**"I've paid"**

does NOT make the payment successful.

A customer saying:

"I transferred the money"

does NOT make it successful.

A screenshot does NOT make it successful.

A merchant saying:

"I received it"

does NOT make it successful.

Only verified payment-provider/banking evidence and TS Pay backend processing can make the payment successful.

# PART L — TS CODES

TS Commerce uses codes for identification.

Example:

### Store Code

`TS-STORE-847291`

Identifies a store.

### Customer Code

`TS-CUST-482917`

Identifies a customer.

### Merchant Admin Payment Code

`TS-MA-739284`

Identifies the merchant's payment/accounting relationship.

### Global TS Admin Code

`TS-ADMIN-928374`

Identifies TS Commerce's administrative payment/accounting relationship.

These are NOT:

* Bank accounts
* Virtual accounts
* Wallet addresses
* Payment destinations
* Passwords
* Payment credentials

Never tell customers to transfer money to one of these codes.

---

# PART M — MERCHANT PAYING TS COMMERCE

When a merchant owes TS Commerce money, for example:

**$30 subscription**

the merchant needs an actual payment method.

If bank transfer is offered:

TS Pay must obtain the actual bank/virtual account/payment destination from the configured payment infrastructure.

For example:

**Bank:** provider-returned bank

**Account:** provider-returned account

**Amount:** actual amount due

**Reference:** merchant/subscription payment reference

The merchant transfers money to the actual payment destination.

The reference identifies the obligation.

The reference itself is NOT the bank account.

---

# PART N — MERCHANT SUBSCRIPTION

Merchant subscription:

**$30/month**

If the merchant has sufficient eligible earnings:

Deduct:

**$30**

If the merchant has only $12 available:

Deduct:

**$12**

Remaining subscription obligation:

**$18**

If the merchant later earns $10:

Deduct:

**$10**

Remaining:

**$8**

If the merchant later earns $50:

Deduct:

**$8**

Remaining merchant funds:

**$42**

Never over-deduct.

Never make the balance negative merely because the subscription is due.

# PART O — REFERRAL SYSTEM

Now add the referral program.

This is a merchant referral program.

The exact rule is:

**A merchant who successfully pays their $30 monthly subscription receives a referral code for that paid referral period.**

If they refer a genuinely new merchant and that new merchant successfully pays their first $30 subscription:

**The referring merchant gets 30% off their next $30 subscription.**

30% of $30:

**$9**

Therefore:

**Next subscription = $21**

---

# PART P — REFERRAL CODE GENERATION

After a merchant's $30 subscription has been successfully verified:

Generate a unique referral code.

Example:

`TS-REF-8K4M92XQ`

The code must be:

* Unique
* Random
* Cryptographically secure
* Server-generated
* Associated with the merchant
* Associated with a subscription period
* Associated with an expiration time

The referral code is valid only during its assigned monthly referral period.

Do not allow expired codes to work.

---

# PART Q — NEW MONTH

When the merchant successfully begins their next paid subscription period:

Create a new referral period/code.

Preserve the old referral record for historical purposes.

Do not destroy old referral data.

Do not allow two conflicting active referral codes for the same merchant and period.

---

# PART R — REFERRAL EXAMPLE

Merchant A pays:

**$30**

TS Commerce generates:

`TS-REF-8K4M92XQ`

Merchant A shares it.

Merchant B signs up using that referral code.

Merchant B pays:

**$30**

TS Pay verifies Merchant B's payment.

The referral engine verifies:

* Merchant B is genuinely eligible
* Code was valid when used
* Merchant B is new
* Merchant B has not previously qualified
* Merchant A is eligible
* Payment is genuine
* Payment is not duplicated
* No fraud rule blocks the reward

Then:

**Merchant A earns a $9 subscription discount.**

At Merchant A's next eligible subscription:

Original:

**$30**

Referral discount:

**−$9**

Amount due:

**$21**

---

# PART S — VERY IMPORTANT: THE REFERRAL IS NOT CASH

The $9 is NOT deposited into Merchant A's withdrawable balance.

It cannot be:

* Withdrawn
* Transferred
* Converted into cash
* Used to pay unrelated fees
* Sent to another merchant

It is a restricted:

**subscription discount entitlement**

---

# PART T — REFERRAL PAYMENT VERIFICATION

Do NOT give Merchant A the $9 merely because Merchant B:

* Clicked the link
* Entered the code
* Created an account
* Started checkout
* Added a card
* Started a bank transfer
* Uploaded a screenshot

The reward is triggered only after Merchant B's qualifying $30 subscription payment has been successfully verified by TS Pay.

---

# PART U — SELF-REFERRAL PROTECTION

Merchant A must not create Merchant B simply to receive the $9 reward.

Protect against:

* Same account
* Same merchant identity
* Multiple emails
* Recreated accounts
* Multiple stores
* Circular referrals
* Fake accounts
* Payment-account relationships
* Suspicious device/account patterns
* Repeated account creation

Do not use IP address alone as proof of fraud.

Multiple legitimate people can share an IP address.

Use multiple legitimate risk signals.

Suspicious cases should be flagged for review.

---

# PART V — ONE REWARD PER NEW MERCHANT

Merchant B's first qualifying subscription can generate only:

**ONE $9 referral reward**

for Merchant A.

Do not allow:

Payment webhook #1 → $9

Payment webhook #2 → another $9

Payment retry → another $9

Page refresh → another $9

API replay → another $9

Account recreation → another $9

Use database uniqueness and idempotency.

---

# PART W — REFUNDS AND CHARGEBACKS

Suppose Merchant B pays $30.

Merchant A receives a $9 referral entitlement.

Later Merchant B's payment is refunded or charged back.

TS Commerce must check whether the referral reward is still valid.

If the reward was not legitimately earned anymore:

* Mark the reward appropriately
* Reverse/recover it where appropriate
* Do not erase history
* Create a compensating ledger/discount record

Do not simply edit the original record.

---

# PART X — REFERRAL EXPIRATION

The referral code expires according to the assigned monthly referral period.

The frontend must NOT determine expiration.

The backend determines:

* Current server time
* Referral period
* Code status
* Expiration
* Eligibility

An expired code cannot generate a new qualifying referral.

However, a referral that was legitimately attributed while the code was valid should remain linked to its original referral record.

# PART Z — REFERRAL + SUBSCRIPTION CALCULATION

The system must calculate:

**Subscription price**

minus

**valid referral discount**

equals

**actual amount due**

Example:

$30

− $9

=

$21

Do not:

1. Charge $30
2. Give $9 as cash
3. Create a random balance
4. Call the subscription $21 without recording the discount

The ledger must clearly show:

**$30 gross subscription**

**−$9 referral discount**

**=$21 payable**

# PART AA — PAYMENT + REFERRAL SECURITY

The frontend cannot decide:

* Whether a merchant paid
* Whether a referral code is valid
* Whether a referral expired
* Whether someone is a new merchant
* Whether a referral qualifies
* Whether a reward exists
* Whether the reward is $9
* Whether the subscription is $21

The backend decides all of these.

# FINAL INSTRUCTION TO REPLIT AGENT

Do not interpret this as a request to merely create UI.

Build the underlying business logic.

For every payment feature, ask:

**Where does the real money go?**

**Who provides the real payment destination?**

**How does TS Pay know the payment actually happened?**

**How does the backend verify it?**

**How is it recorded in the ledger?**

**How does the merchant balance change?**

**What happens if the payment is duplicated?**

**What happens if the payment is reversed?**

**What happens if the provider is unavailable?**

For every referral feature, ask:

**Who owns the referral code?**

**When does it become valid?**

**When does it expire?**

**Who actually qualifies?**

**Has the referred merchant genuinely paid?**

**Can the same payment create two rewards?**

**Can someone refer themselves?**

**Can the $9 become cash accidentally?**

**Can a refund make the reward invalid?**

If the answer to any of these is unclear, do not guess.

Inspect the existing architecture and implement the safest correct design.

Do not create fake financial behavior merely to make the UI appear functional.
